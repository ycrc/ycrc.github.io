# NHCE (Hopper) Rules of Behavior

**NIST SP 800-171 and HIPAA Compliant Environments**
Version 0.7.4

---

## 1. Background

The NIST SP 800-171 and HIPAA Compliant Environments (NHCE) cyberinfrastructure systems serve Yale researchers and the greater scientific community. Comprised of physical (Hopper HPC Cluster, aka NHCE-MG) and virtual (Spinup+, aka NHCE-AWS) infrastructure, the NHCE provides a range of services and capabilities to carry out research on sensitive data. NHCE systems must be properly managed and maintained to ensure their continual, secure functioning.

To this end, all personnel working with NHCE systems must review and agree to this Rules of Behavior (RoB) document before access to either the NHCE-AWS or NHCE-MG is granted. Moreover, should these rules be revised or updated, personnel who have agreed to the previous iteration must review and agree to the new iteration.

This RoB is comprised of two components:

1. Yale University's [Information Technology Appropriate Use Policy](https://your.yale.edu/policies-procedures/policies/1607-information-technology-appropriate-use-policy) and
2. The additional rules herein, which apply to all NHCE users

By reviewing this RoB, you agree to adhere to both components.

## 2. Yale's Information Technology Appropriate Use Policy

The University's IT Appropriate Use Policy may be found at: [https://your.yale.edu/policies-procedures/policies/1607-information-technology-appropriate-use-policy](docs/clusters/hopper-rob.md.

In addition to Yale's IT Appropriate Use Policy, the following rules must be agreed to and followed for a user to access the NHCE.

## 3. Authorized Use

NHCE system resources shall only be utilized for authorized research and other business purposes, and not for personal or recreational activities.

If someone no longer requires access to the NHCE (e.g., no longer working on an NHCE project, leaving Yale University), they must alert the appropriate System Owner either through their supervisor or directly. This alert must happen within one business day of the changed access need.

## 4. Access Control

Users shall use passwords, multifactor authentication, and encryption to protect the confidentiality of sensitive information, including Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) and/or electronic Protected Health Information (ePHI). Users must not share access credentials or authorized portable media with anyone else.

If a user expects their session to be idle at least 20 minutes, they must preemptively log out. Users must also log out when their session's work is complete.

## 5. Software Use

Only authorized software may be installed and used on NHCE resources. Users must not install or use any software that is not previously authorized by the relevant System Owners.

## 6. Data Protection

Users are responsible for protecting the confidentiality of data, including CUI and ePHI, by following appropriate procedures with respect to the processing, storage and transmission of these data. All electronic CUI and ePHI must be encrypted when in flight and at rest. Screensharing and screenshots are prohibited. Physical materials (e.g., paper) containing CUI and ePHI must be stored in locked drawers/cabinets within secure office facilities. Users shall not share CUI/ePHI data with unauthorized parties and will abide by the provisions of all relevant University policies, standards, and procedures.

Suspected incidents are to be reported in a timely manner to the ISO via the [Report an Incident](https://cybersecurity.yale.edu/get-help/report/report-incident) webpage or by contacting [YU's Help Desk](https://yale.service-now.com/it?id=get_help). Alternatively, ePHI incidents are to be reported immediately to YU's HIPAA Security Officer Hotline (203-627-4665).

Should users, system administrators, or system owners be unable to operate from their designated workplace (e.g., their office at YU), they must find alternate accommodations. Such accommodations will meet or exceed the security requirements of the designated workplace. ([YALE-MSS-4.1](https://cybersecurity.yale.edu/mss/4/1)]) This will entail a separate, lockable room where the user can work, along with the arrangement of display devices so as to prevent visibility from any window. The use of privacy screens or other visual restrictions may be employed as an alternate means to block display visibility. The user is not permitted to work in a public space, such as a coffee shop, restaurant, airport terminal, etc. Should the user be unable to find any suitable alternate accommodations, they must cease work and contact their supervisor or system owner for assistance.

## 7. Network Use

Users shall not engage in any activity that interferes with the normal operation of the NHCE's network infrastructure or jeopardizes its security. This includes, but is not limited to, using the NHCE network for unauthorized or illegal purposes, attempting to gain unauthorized access to other systems, or distributing malware.

## 8. Email Use

Users shall only use Yale-approved email systems associated with their assigned accounts to send or receive emails containing CUI/ePHI. The use of third-party email systems is prohibited (e.g. Yahoo or Gmail).

## 9. Media Handling

### A. General Prohibition of Portable Media

Except as noted below, the use of portable, removable media (such as USB flash drives) is prohibited for systems where CUI/ePHI reside, or through which such data flow. These media 1) make it possible to surreptitiously move data onto and off devices, and 2) generally do not include malware countermeasures.

### B. Portable Media Exceptions

Media use by NHCE users is only allowed by approved exception. This can be in the form of in a technology control plan (TCP), wherein the relevant System Owner outlines the use of media with respect to their data.

### C. Sanitization

All media storing CUI or ePHI will be securely sanitized prior to disposal, release from organizational control, or reuse.

### D. Marking

All media containing CUI or ePHI will be marked to indicate distribution limitations, handling caveats, and security classification.

### E. Ownership

The use of media without an identifiable owner is prohibited.

## 10. Social Media and Publicly Accessible Systems

The posting of any sensitive data or NHCE-related system information to social media sites or publicly accessible systems (e.g. departmental webpage, research project website) is prohibited. Such information includes, but is not limited to: CUI, ePHI, NHCE configurations/characteristics (e.g., IP addresses, OS and application names and/or versions, architecture descriptions), security-related NHCE information (e.g., descriptions or discussions of deployed controls), NHCE personnel information, and NHCE project information that is non-public.

A NHCE user that has privileges to publish content on a publicly accessible system is required to review the publicly accessible system yearly for any sensitive data.

## 11. Mobile Devices

Only officially authorized and configured mobile devices (e.g., phones, tablets) are permitted to access NHCE systems. Personally owned mobile devices may only be permitted to access systems by written exception from the NHCE's System Owner.

## 12. Monitoring and Enforcement

### A. Security Reviews

Yale's Information Security Office (ISO) will conduct regular security reviews to ensure compliance with the provisions of these rules.

### B. Handling Compliance Issues

Compliance issues on the part of a user can result in the immediate termination of access. Such issues will be reported to Yale's Chief Information Security Officer and, as needed, the Office of General Counsel and Chief Privacy Officer.

## 13. Review and Updates

### A. Rules Changes

These rules will be reviewed no less than triennially and updated as necessary to address emerging threats and technologies.

### B. User Notification

NHCE users will be notified of major changes to this document and may be required to re-affirm agreement with its provisions to maintain their access to NHCE resources.
